1. Agile Methodology
Term
Meaning
1. Agile
A software development approach in which teams deliver work in small parts and adapt to changing requirements.
2. Scrum
An Agile framework used to manage work in short cycles with defined roles and meetings.
3. Sprint
A fixed period, usually 1–4 weeks, in which a team completes a planned amount of work.
4. Iteration
A repeated development cycle used to build, test, and improve a product.
5. User Story
A short description of a feature from the user's perspective. Example: “As a user, I want to log in securely.”
6. Epic
A large body of work that can be divided into smaller user stories.
7. Story Point
A relative estimate of the effort, complexity, and uncertainty involved in completing a user story.
8. Acceptance Criteria
Conditions that must be satisfied for a user story to be accepted as complete.
2. Scrum Roles — What They Do
Role
Responsibility
PO – Product Owner
Defines product goals, manages and prioritizes the Product Backlog, and maximizes product value.
SM – Scrum Master
Helps the team follow Scrum, facilitates Scrum events, and helps remove impediments.
Developers
Design, develop, test, and deliver a usable product increment during each Sprint.
Stakeholders
Provide feedback, share business needs, and help the team understand expectations.
Note: The Scrum Guide defines three formal accountabilities: Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. Stakeholders are important participants but are not a formal Scrum accountability.
3. Scrum Events and Their Purpose
Event / Term
Purpose
Sprint Planning
The team decides why the Sprint is valuable, what work can be completed, and how to do it.
Daily Scrum
A 15-minute event for Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan.
Sprint Review
The team and stakeholders inspect the completed work and discuss feedback and future changes.
Sprint Retrospective
The Scrum Team discusses what went well, what could improve, and how to work better in the next Sprint.
Sprint Backlog
The Sprint Goal, selected Product Backlog items, and the plan for delivering them during the Sprint.
Sprint Goal
The single objective that gives the Sprint a clear purpose.
Important: Sprint Backlog and Sprint Goal are Scrum concepts, not Scrum events. The Sprint is the main container for the Scrum events.
